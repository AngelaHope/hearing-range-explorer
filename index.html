Where in your ear does a sound land?

Your cochlea is a snail-shaped tube lined with about 15,000 hair cells. High pitches shake the hair cells near its entrance; low pitches travel all the way to the centre. Play a tone and watch where it lands.

From 20 Hz (a deep rumble) to 20,000 Hz (a thin whine).

Reading your result

Healthy young ears
The textbook range is roughly 20 to 20,000 Hz. Few adults reach either end.
Age
The hair cells at the cochlea's entrance take the most wear, so the top of the range shrinks with age. This is called presbycusis.
Noise
Loud music and machinery damage hair cells, often first around 3,000 to 6,000 Hz. Hair cells in humans don't grow back.
Your equipment
Many phone and laptop speakers can't play below about 100 Hz or above about 16,000 Hz. A "not heard" at the extremes may be your speaker, not your ear.

This is a learning tool, not a medical hearing test. If you're worried about your hearing, see an audiologist.
